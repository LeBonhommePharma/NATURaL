## App Review notes

No account is required. On first launch, read the welcome screen and select “Find my first session”. Select “Begin a gentle session” to open the workout preparation screen. All sessions are available.

Health access is optional under the information button → About & Privacy → Choose Health permissions. Clinical medication reads have a separate consent flow in Prescriptions. Without shared samples, metrics can be unavailable; guided movement remains usable. Entropy indicators are experimental wellness features, not clinical diagnosis or drug-binding measurements.

Records stay on the device. NATURaL does not collect user data; analysis runs locally. There is no account and no iCloud sync.

The embedded Watch app can start sessions on the wrist and requests Health permissions there. A paired physical Apple Watch is needed to validate live heart-rate collection.

Before pasting these notes into App Store Connect, verify the final build and complete the remaining gates in README.md. Supply an actual reviewer phone number.

## Native tvOS listing — standalone sessions and optional phone pairing

Use this copy for `com.natural.Bonhomme.tv` after the tvOS build and physical Apple TV checks pass. Standalone TV sessions do not record Health workouts or save a session history.

English subtitle: Free guided movement on TV

English description:

Make room for movement on the big screen. Choose a free chair-yoga plan with your Apple TV remote, select Begin Session, and follow a colourful pose illustration, step-by-step guidance, breathing cues and countdowns. Pause, resume or end whenever you need. No iPhone or account is required for standalone sessions.

Want to display an iPhone session instead? Choose Pair iPhone, scan the temporary invitation, and confirm sharing on your phone. Your iPhone controls that session; the TV displays the guide and any available measurements shared by the phone. Standalone TV sessions do not measure heart rate or SCI, record workouts to Apple Health, or save session history.

Use a stable chair and move within a comfortable range. The illustrations guide movement; they do not assess your posture or provide medical advice.
